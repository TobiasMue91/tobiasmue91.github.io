About Conway's Game of Life

John Conway's Game of Life (1970) is a grid of cells that are alive or dead. Each generation, every cell counts its eight neighbours: a dead cell with exactly three live neighbours comes alive, a live cell with two or three stays alive, and every other cell dies or stays dead. That rule is written B3/S23; other Life-like rules change the two lists of numbers.

From those rules come still lifes that never change, oscillators that repeat, spaceships that travel (the glider moves one cell diagonally every four generations, written c/4), guns that fire spaceships for ever, and small patterns such as the R-pentomino that churn for over a thousand generations before settling. Patterns are shared as RLE text, the format LifeWiki and Golly use; this page reads and writes it.

Runs entirely in your browser; your pattern is kept on this device only. Rules follow LifeWiki's B/S notation; patterns from LifeWiki paste as they are. gptgames.dev
